# Essential Work Must Continue When the Network Does Not.

> Critical knowledge and coordination should not disappear because one cloud service, vendor or connection is unavailable.

## The problem

Organizations increasingly depend on centralized networks for payments, records, communication and workflow. A cyberattack or outage can remove not only automation but the information people need to continue manually.

## What breaks

- Central Dependency
- Connectivity Failure
- Vendor Dependency
- Continuity Gap

## Who holds part of the picture

- patient
- family
- clinicians
- care coordinators

## With Koali

Koali treats portable knowledge, local copies, offline workflows, synchronization and explicit degraded-mode procedures as part of the operating model. Critical cases can continue locally and reconcile later.

## Human authority

Authorized humans and institutions retain their existing legal, professional and democratic authority. Koali structures knowledge, coordination and traceability; it does not take sovereign or professional decisions.

## What success looks like

- The relevant state remains visible across handoffs and time.
- Responsibilities, unresolved questions and escalation paths are explicit.
- The outcome produces reusable memory for the next comparable case.

## Real-world parallels

### Change Healthcare Cybersecurity Incident — U.S. HHS

HHS described the 2024 cyberattack as having unprecedented nationwide impact on patients and health-care providers.

**Relation to scenario:** `DIRECT`

### U.S. GAO — Critical Legacy Systems

GAO identified critical federal legacy systems 8 to 51 years old, including outdated languages, unsupported technology and known vulnerabilities.

**Relation to scenario:** `STRONG ANALOGUE`  
**Source:** https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-19-471

## Boundary

The documented cases illustrate analogous failure mechanisms. They do not establish that Koali would have prevented the event or guaranteed a different outcome.
